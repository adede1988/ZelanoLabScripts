**The question was:** which measures have *low between-control variability* **and** at least one of — (1) cleanly separate controls from Dupi, (2) separate responders from non-responders, (3) track olfactory recovery. Here is the honest answer after external review.

**1. The clean, robust result is behavioral, not (yet) gamma.** The olfactory **composite index** separates controls (mean 0.64) from Dupi (S1 0.09 → S2 0.31 → S3 0.26) and shows a clear session-1→session-2 rise — i.e. it *tracks recovery* and *separates the cohorts*. This is what defines the responder split (9 responders, 2 non-responders, 2 unclassified) and is the trustworthy backbone of the study.

**2. No single gamma measure yet clears the bar.** With session-level bootstrap CIs, **0 of 460** control↔Dupi gamma separations have a 95% CI excluding zero, and the same holds after peak-gating and after airflow regression. The eye-catching "goodness ≈ 2" measures in the first pass were driven by (a) the n=2 non-responder group, (b) ridge-frequency's mechanically low CV (a bounded-tracker artifact, since the ridge is confined to 25–58 Hz), and (c) no multiple-comparison control — all three flagged independently by the reviewers and confirmed here. **Ridge-frequency and burst-count measures should not be treated as biomarkers**: the FOOOF-selected channel has a real periodic gamma peak in only 39% of recordings, so on the other 61% these measures describe the 1/f background, and a broadband-transient simulation reproduces the wandering mid-band ridge with no oscillation present.

**3. The most promising *direction* — underpowered but consistent — is reduced gamma power + steeper 1/f in Dupi.** The largest point-effects are **absolute ridge/band power in dB** (`w4/w5_rpowDb`, `w4/w5_bandDb`), Dupi *lower* than control (d ≈ −1.6 to −1.9 across cue/O15/thresh), and these **partially survive** controlling for the aperiodic exponent (e.g. threshTask `w5_rpowZ` d = −1.30 → −1.00), so the gap is not purely a 1/f artifact. Independently, Dupi show a **steeper aperiodic exponent** than controls (d ≈ 0.28–0.79), an excitation/inhibition-type difference in its own right. Both are the right things to power up.

**4. Low control-CV alone is a trap.** The measures with the lowest between-control variability (ridge frequency ~42 Hz, CV ~0.045; `coup_inhExhRatio`, CV ~0.02) are low-variance for the *wrong* reasons — a band-bounded estimator and an airflow-driven ratio, respectively. Low control variance must be paired with a demonstrated oscillation and a confound-controlled separation before it means anything.

**Bottom line for a biomarker.** Behaviorally, olfactory recovery is real and measurable. Neurally, the honest current status is *promising direction, not yet a biomarker*: **lower peri-inhalation gamma power and a steeper aperiodic exponent in Dupi vs controls**, both underpowered and both needing the follow-up the reviewers specified — electrode localization to the olfactory pathway (inside>outside specificity), a larger non-responder sample, per-breath peak-gating as the primary filter, evoked-vs-induced separation, surrogate nulls for respiration–gamma coupling, and within-subject test-retest reliability. Those steps, not another descriptive metric, are what would turn the direction into a biomarker.
