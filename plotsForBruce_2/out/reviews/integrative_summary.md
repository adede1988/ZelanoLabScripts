**The question was:** which measures have *low between-control variability* **and** at least one of — (1) cleanly separate controls from Dupi, (2) separate responders from non-responders, (3) track olfactory recovery. Here is the honest answer.

**Classification note.** Responders are now defined by *sustained recovery*: a participant is a **responder** only if their composite at the **final recorded session** is ≥ 0.40 (≈⅔ of the control mean, 0.64) **and** exceeds their session-1 value; otherwise **non-responder**; a single-session participant is unclassified. This correctly labels the four participants who fell back at session 3 (GH, TB, KS, JN) as non-responders even though several rose at session 2. Result: **4 responders (JH, AB, BS, PD), 8 non-responders, 1 unclassified (DL)** — which also removes the earlier n=2 non-responder fragility.

**1. The clean, robust result is behavioral.** The olfactory **composite** separates controls (0.64) from Dupi (S1 0.09 → S2 0.31 → S3 0.26) and rises S1→S2. In the per-participant line plots, responders (solid) climb toward the control band while non-responders (dashed) fall back by session 3.

**2. Controls vs Dupi in gamma: no single measure clears the bar.** With session-level bootstrap CIs, **0 of 460** control↔Dupi gamma separations have a 95% CI excluding zero — under raw, peak-gated, and airflow-adjusted analyses alike (controls n=5–19 sessions). The largest *point* effects are **absolute ridge/band power in dB, lower in Dupi** (`w4/w5_rpowDb`, d ≈ −1.8), which **partially survive** 1/f control (e.g. threshTask `w5_rpowZ` −1.30 → −1.00). Dupi also show a **steeper aperiodic exponent** than controls (d ≈ 0.28–0.79). Ridge-frequency/burst measures remain untrustworthy (39% peak-detection rate; the broadband-transient simulation reproduces a wandering mid-band ridge with no oscillation).

**3. Responder vs non-responder — moderate, and partly confounded by breathing.** With the balanced groups (≈18 vs 10 sessions), the largest responder↔non-responder point-separations are the **aperiodic exponent** (threshTask d=1.53, cueTask d=1.02), the **per-breath periodic gamma bump `gammaBumpDb`** (O15 d=1.16, cueTask d=1.00), and **peakZ** (threshTask d=1.03, control CV 0.045). *However*, respiration **timing** also separates the groups strongly (cueTask/O15 `returnCrossMs`/`exhaleStartMs` d≈1.0–1.2) — i.e. responders and non-responders **breathe differently** — so a gamma difference may partly be an airflow difference (Zelano's confound). Consistent with this, the airflow-adjusted re-ranking yields no CI-significant control↔Dupi separations.

**4. Low control-CV alone remains a trap.** The lowest-CV measures (ridge frequency ~42 Hz, CV ~0.045; `coup_inhExhRatio`, CV ~0.02) are low-variance for the wrong reasons — a band-bounded estimator and an airflow-driven ratio.

**Bottom line.** Behaviorally, olfactory recovery is real and cleanly measured. Neurally, the most promising, mutually-consistent signals are **(a) lower peri-inhalation gamma power + steeper 1/f in Dupi vs controls**, and **(b) higher aperiodic exponent and a stronger per-breath periodic gamma bump in responders vs non-responders** — all moderate, underpowered, and — critically — needing the breathing (airflow) confound removed before they can be called biomarkers, plus electrode localization to the olfactory pathway, per-breath peak-gating as the primary filter, surrogate nulls for coupling, and within-subject reliability.
